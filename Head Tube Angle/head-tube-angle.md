# Head Tube Angle (MTB Steering Geometry)

## Definition

**Head tube angle (HTA)** is the angle between the ground and the bike’s **steering axis** (the line through the head tube / fork steerer). It’s usually quoted at normal ride height; suspension sag and setup can shift the *effective* angle slightly.

- **Steeper** (bigger number, e.g. 70°): steering axis closer to vertical
- **Slacker** (smaller number, e.g. 63°): steering axis leans back more

```
Steep (≈90°)            Slack (≈63°)
    |                       \
    |                        \
____|____ ground         _____\____ ground
```

## Typical numbers

| Bike type | Typical HTA |
|---|---|
| Road / gravel | 71–74° |
| XC | 67–69° |
| Trail | 64–67° |
| Enduro | 63–65° |
| Downhill | 62–64° |

## What it changes (ride feel)

HTA mostly changes **trail** (and therefore steering feel).

**Trail** is the horizontal distance between:
1) where the steering axis hits the ground, and
2) the tire contact patch.

- **Slacker (↓HTA)** → typically **more trail** → stronger self-centering → more stability at speed and in rough terrain, but slower/heavier steering at low speed.
- **Steeper (↑HTA)** → typically **less trail** → quicker turn-in and tighter handling, but more nervous/twitchy when speeds and impacts go up.

Other effects you feel:

- **Descending / high speed:** slacker = calmer, less deflection; steeper = more “busy”.
- **Steep climbing:** steeper = easier to keep the front wheel tracking; slacker = more wandering / front gets light.
- **Tight switchbacks:** steeper = easier to pivot; slacker = wants to hold a straighter line.

## “Why do bikes need to be steep and slack?”

They don’t need to be both at once. HTA is a **trade-off**:

- Bikes optimized for **climbing and low-speed precision** (XC, some trail) choose **steeper** HTA.
- Bikes optimized for **descending and stability** (enduro, DH) choose **slacker** HTA.

Some frames let you adjust HTA (flip chips, angle headsets) to move slightly toward one side of the trade-off.

## Related geometry (what interacts with HTA)

- **Fork offset (rake):** more offset generally reduces trail (quicker steering); less offset increases trail (more stable).
- **Wheelbase / reach:** longer = more stable; often paired with slacker HTA.
- **Seat tube angle:** often steeper on modern bikes to keep climbing position centered while HTA goes slack for descending.
