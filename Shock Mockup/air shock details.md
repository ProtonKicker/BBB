# Air Shock Series — What Each One Is For

RockShox makes four air-sprung rear shock series, and they are sorted by **the job the bike does**, not by price. Picking a shock therefore starts with the frame, not the component list.

This document explains each series, then makes a pick for our build (see [README.md](README.md)):

> Brand: Scram — Type: Airshock — for: Short travel trail bike — budget not a concern.

Sizing data for every model: [air shock stroke.md](air%20shock%20stroke.md). Full model/part-number roster with prices: [air shock roster.md](air%20shock%20roster.md).

## How to read a RockShox shock before you compare them

| Thing | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `185x52.5` | Eye-to-eye × stroke, both in millimetres. Eye-to-eye is the mounting length; **stroke** is how far the shaft moves. |
| `(TR)` | Trunnion eyelet (metric shaft-hardware mount). Everything without `(TR)` is a standard round eyelet. |
| Series name | The discipline the chassis and valving are built for — SIDLuxe, Deluxe, Super Deluxe, Vivid. |
| Tier suffix | Equipment level *within* a series: (base) → Select → Select+ → Ultimate → Ultimate Flight Attendant. |
| Damper code | Codes as they appear in SRAM's *Damper Type* row: `R` rebound · `L` lockout / pedalling mode · `C` low-speed compression · `T` an extra compression circuit (Throttle on Super Deluxe and Vivid) · the number after `C` = clicks of compression. `Hydraulic Bottom Out` / `Adj. Hydraulic Bottom Out` = the damping curve in the last ~20 % of the stroke, sometimes externally adjustable. The `Rebound tune`, `Compression tune` and `Lockout force` rows on each model page list the actual adjusters — check there rather than trusting the code. |
| Stroke ≠ frame travel | Frame travel = stroke × the frame's leverage ratio (roughly 2.5–3.3:1 on modern trail bikes). So a 45 mm-stroke shock is normally a **~110–135 mm** frame, and a 65 mm-stroke shock is normally **~170 mm+**. The frame spec, not the shock catalogue, decides which line you buy. |

General-knowledge note: the frame-travel figures in this document are ordinary industry approximations. The size, weight, damper and mount figures are scraped from SRAM's own pages — see *Where the numbers come from* at the end.

## SIDLuxe — cross-country and downcountry

RockShox's own banner for the series is **"SMALL AND MIGHTY"**, and the model copy is explicit about the terrain: *"Blistering sprints, chunky descents, and high-speed singletrack — SIDLuxe Ultimate is built for this."*

- **What it's for:** the lightest, most pedalling-efficient air shock RockShox makes. Lockout-oriented damping (2-position and the new 3-position Open / Pedal / Lock platforms) rather than big-hit cushioning.
- **Bikes it fits:** XC and downcountry full-suspension bikes paired with a SID or SID SL fork — Scott Spark, Specialized Epic, Trek Top Fuel and Supercaliber, Canyon Lux, Giant Anthem.
- **Size envelope:** standard shocks run eye-to-eye 145 → 210 mm, stroke **27.5 → 50 mm** — the shortest strokes in the range, i.e. roughly **90–120 mm** frames. The special formats sit outside that (215x40, 239x40).
- **Weight (published):** 246–252 g for the 2P/3P Ultimate and Select+ models (measured "Remote … In or Out 190x45 Solo Air 0 Tokens"); 349 g for the Flight Attendant (190x45, no battery). Nothing else here is lighter.
- **Dampers:** `RL`, `RL3`, `RLR` (remote lockout), plus `RC4` on the Specialized-specific WCID.
- **Special formats:** IsoStrut (`239x40`, Trek Top Fuel / Supercaliber), Scott Spark-specific Flight Attendant (`165x45(TR)` only), and WCID (`215x40`, Epic World Cup). These are frame-locked parts, not generic replacements.

**Verdict for use:** right answer if the bike leans XC — 100–110 mm, a lockout you actually use, and every gram counted. It is *not* the cushioning-first option.

## Deluxe — trail

Banner: **"PROVEN TRAIL PERFORMANCE"**. RockShox describes it as *"the most balanced shock we make, focusing on an equal parts climbing efficiency and descending capabilities"*, and the Ultimate as *"an inline shock with all the tuning options needed for precise ride performance, light weight, and bottom-out control."*

- **What it's for:** the general-purpose trail shock. Inline body (no external piggyback reservoir), so it disappears into tight linkages, with a real compression adjuster instead of a lockout-only platform.
- **Bikes it fits:** short-to-mid-travel trail bikes — the 120–140 mm class (Specialized Stumpjumper and Epic EVO, Trek Fuel EX, Santa Cruz Hightower, Transition Relay, Norco Fluid, Scott Genius). These examples are illustrative; the frame's own shock spec is what governs.
- **Size envelope:** eye-to-eye 145 → 230 mm, stroke **30 → 65 mm** — from the smallest 145 mm downcountry mounts up to light-enduro `230x65`. That is the widest useful coverage of any series here.
- **Weight (published):** 370 g (Select) / 376 g (Select+) / 378 g (Ultimate), all "210x55, no hardware installed".
- **Dampers:** `R` (Select: rebound only) · `RL`, `RT` (Select+) · `RCT` (Ultimate — its spec page lists rebound tunes R25/R55/R85, six compression tunes C22–C40 and lockout tunes X4/X6/X8, i.e. all three adjusted separately) · Scott-specific `RL3` / `RLC3` (Nude, with Descend / Traction Control / Lock modes).
- **Tuning extras:** independent low-speed compression is the headline feature — the thing that makes a short-travel bike feel supported without being harsh.

**Verdict for use:** this is the series engineered for exactly the category we're building.

## Super Deluxe — trail and enduro

Banner: **"TRAIL PERFECTED"**. The C2 Ultimate copy reads *"Give your all-day trail bike the firepower it needs for rugged terrain, plus the efficiency to climb back up for more."* The D1 generation adds the LinearXL air spring and Hydraulic Bottom Out, which *"kicks in within the last 20 % of travel, sparing you the pain of that harsh clanking bottom out after impact without sacrificing reactivity or traction."*

- **What it's for:** the higher-volume, more-adjustable enduro counterpart — wider compression range and bottom-out control as first-class features, plus the LinearXL spring on the newer generation.
- **Bikes it fits:** 140–170 mm trail/enduro frames — Santa Cruz Bronson and Tallboy, Trek Slash, Specialized Enduro and Levo, Yeti SB140/SB160, plus e-MTBs (all listed E-bike Approved).
- **Size envelope:** eye-to-eye 165 → 230 mm, stroke **37.5 → 65 mm**.
- **Weight (published):** 445–458 g across the C2 base/Select/Select+/Ultimate, 560 g for the C2 Flight Attendant, and 528–530 g on the D1 generation.
- **Dampers:** `R` (base) · `RT` (Select) · `RCT` (Select+) · `RC2T` (Ultimate — rebound, 2-position compression, Throttle) · `RC3` (Flight Attendant) · most also offered with `Hydraulic Bottom Out`.
- **Genie variants:** `RS-SDLX-GNUL-D1` (Ultimate Genie) only in **190x45 and 210x55**. Genie is RockShox's position-sensitive air spring that gives more travel at lower shaft force; Select Genie has no published SRAM page yet, so treat it as unproven stock.

**Verdict for use:** a legitimate option if the frame *specifies* a Super Deluxe size or has clearance for a reservoir, but you pay ~70–180 g over Deluxe for cushioning a short-travel bike cannot use.

## Vivid — downhill / gravity

Banner: **"GRAVITY EVOLVED"**; copy: *"World Cup-proven and built to charge… The LinearXL air spring delivers a coil-like feel"*, with an Adjustable Bottom Out dial offering five settings in the last 20 % of shock travel.

- **What it's for:** downhill race and big-gravity riding — maximum heat capacity, maximum adjustment, maximum travel consumed.
- **Bikes it fits:** 200 mm downhill frames (Trek Session, Giant Glory, Santa Cruz V10, Specialized Demo) and the biggest enduro bikes.
- **Size envelope:** eye-to-eye 165 → 250 mm, stroke **37.5 → 75 mm**. It sells the same short lines as Deluxe and Super Deluxe (`165x37.5(TR)`, `190x45`), but the spring curve, oil volume and valving behind those sizes are built to be eaten on DH runs.
- **Weight (published):** 657 g (base) to 785 g (Flight Attendant) — roughly double a Deluxe.
- **Dampers:** `R` · `RT` · `RCT` · `RC2` / `RC2T` (Ultimate, plus a dedicated Ultimate DH) · `RC3` (Flight Attendant), all with `Adj. Hydraulic Bottom Out`.

**Verdict for use:** wrong tool. Short travel frames cannot supply the oil volumes, spring rates or clearance these are built around.

# What fits:  short-travel trail bike

The build is a short-travel trail bike — call it **~100–130 mm rear travel** — air-sprung only, budget open. That fixes two things before any component preference matters:

1. **Stroke should land at 37.5–50 mm** on a typical 2.6–3.1:1 trail linkage (37.5 → ~100–115 mm, 42.5–45 → ~110–140 mm, 50 → ~130–155 mm).
2. **The chassis should be inline, light, and valved for small hits** — a short-travel frame has no spare travel to absorb a harsh, high-volume downhill tune, and every extra 100 g is a bigger percentage of a light bike.

### The pick — Deluxe Ultimate, `RS-DLX-ULT-C2`

The series is built for this category, and within it the Ultimate is the version worth having when budget is not a constraint:

- **`RCT` damper** — rebound, independent low-speed compression and a lockout. On 100–130 mm the LSC adjuster is the difference between a bike that wallows under braking and one that holds its line; the Select tier (`R`, rebound only) gives away the half of the tune that short travel needs most.
- **Lightest of the trail-tier shocks:** 378 g published (at 210x55) — 80–150 g below a Super Deluxe Ultimate, and 130 g more than a SIDLuxe Ultimate 3P but with a genuine descending adjuster instead of a lockout.
- **Inline body, no reservoir,** so it clears the tight linkages that short-travel frames actually use.
- **24 published sizes** from 165x37.5(TR) to 230x65 (the Select and Select+ widen the family down to 145x35(TR) and 170x30/35), including both mount types — `165x37.5/40/42.5/45(TR)`, `185x47.5/50/52.5/55(TR)`, `190x37.5/40/42.5/45`, `205x57.5–65(TR)`, `210x47.5–55`, `230x57.5–65`. Whatever the frame spec says, Deluxe almost certainly sells that line.
- Every Deluxe model in the roster carries SRAM's **E-bike Approved** flag, so the chassis is not the limitation if the frame ever ends up mid-motor.

Order it in **exactly the eye-to-eye × stroke and mount type your frame manufacturer prints** — read it off the shock yoke or the frame spec sheet, never from a bike-review article. For a short-travel trail frame that will most likely be one of the `165x37.5–45(TR)`, `185x47.5–52.5(TR)` or `190x37.5–45` lines. At the same time match body-eyelet width and hardware (through-bolt length, bushings vs bearings), then set progression with DebonAir tokens or a MegNeg rather than by guessing air pressure. `ShockWiz` is free tuning guidance once it is mounted.

### Second choices, and when they beat the pick

- **SIDLuxe Ultimate 3P, `RS-SIDL-ULT3-A2`** — if the bike is really ~100–110 mm, paired with a SID fork, and you want 247 g with Open/Pedal/Lock plus a remote. Lighter and more efficient than the Deluxe; less compression support when the trail gets rough.
- **Super Deluxe Ultimate C2, `RS-SDLX-ULT-C2`** — if the frame spec calls out a size or mount that Deluxe doesn't publish, or the bike leans closer to 150 mm than 120. `RC2T` plus Hydraulic Bottom Out at 458 g. Pick this only when the frame asks for it.
- **Any `…Flight Attendant` model** — budget is open, so this is tempting, but SRAM's published weights put Flight Attendant about 100–115 g above the equivalent cable-adjustable Ultimate (SIDLuxe 349 vs 247 g; Super Deluxe 560 vs 458 g; Vivid 785 vs 670 g), and the weight figures are quoted *without* the battery the system needs. It also asks for an AxS-based build (battery, pairing, charging) that a one-off short-travel frame is usually not wired for. Skip it here.

### Ruled out regardless of budget

**All Vivid models** (downhill chassis, 657–785 g, valving and spring rates built around 200 mm frames) · **Super Deluxe D1**, whose published weights (528–530 g) buy enduro cushioning a short-travel frame cannot use · **OEM-locked shocks** — Deluxe Nude, Scott Spark SIDLuxe Flight Attendant, SIDLuxe IsoStrut, SIDLuxe WCID and the `RS-DLX-SCTT-C1` Scott RL3 are frame-specific parts, not aftermarket swaps · **every coil-sprung model**, which is out by definition.

**Bottom line:** a short-travel trail bike wants the **Deluxe** chassis at the **Ultimate** tier — `RS-DLX-ULT-C2`, bought in the frame's printed eye-to-eye × stroke and mount type.

## Where the numbers come from

- Series positioning and taglines: RockShox series pages — [SIDLuxe](https://www.sram.com/en/rockshox/series/sidluxe), [Deluxe](https://www.sram.com/en/rockshox/series/deluxe), [Super Deluxe](https://www.sram.com/en/rockshox/series/super-deluxe), [Vivid](https://www.sram.com/en/rockshox/series/vivid).
- Sizes, damper types, eyelets, weights and quoted model copy: each model's SRAM spec table, e.g. [`rs-dlx-ult-c2`](https://www.sram.com/en/rockshox/models/rs-dlx-ult-c2), gathered from the [Rear Shocks listing](https://www.sram.com/en/rockshox/products/rear-shocks?filters=ride|Mountain&sort=Relevancy).
- Frame-travel and leverage-ratio figures are approximations of general suspension practice, not published SRAM claims. Weights are the value SRAM publishes for that model — measured at a stated size, so they shift with the size you buy.
